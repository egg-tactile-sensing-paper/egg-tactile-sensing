\paragraph{Accuracy on eggs the detector has never seen.} Averaged over the 5 folds, the
four detectors reach mAP$_{50}$ 0.868--0.898 from COCO-pretrained weights and 0.837--0.883 from
random initialisation (Table~\ref{tab:main}). Pooling the folds instead of averaging them
gives 0.812--0.849 and 0.765--0.813 (Table~\ref{tab:pooled}). The qualitative claim the
dataset was collected to support therefore holds under a protocol that never scores a model
on an egg it was trained on: a GelSight contact image carries enough signal to localise and
classify a shell crack. What does not hold is the precision with which that result was
previously quoted.

\paragraph{The four detectors are not separable.} Fold-to-fold standard deviation of mAP$_{50}$
is 0.035--0.047 with pretrained weights and 0.023--0.073 from scratch, against a spread
between the detector means of 0.03 and 0.05 respectively. The leading architecture is
YOLOv8m in the first case and YOLOv11m in the second: changing the initialisation reorders
them. Which model wins on this dataset is a statement about which eggs fell in which split,
so we report the four as a baseline range and draw no architectural conclusion. This is the
comparison a single split is least equipped to make and most likely to appear to settle.

\paragraph{The natural-image prior is not doing the work.} Under the grouped protocol,
pretrained initialisation averages 0.880 mAP$_{50}$ against 0.861 from random weights, a gap of
0.02 that sits inside the fold-to-fold variation; on the released split the ordering is the
other way round (0.942 from scratch against 0.919 pretrained). Neither initialisation is
reliably ahead, so the released recipe's 350 epochs from random weights costs nothing in
accuracy, and the appearance of a crack in a gel image is evidently learnable from the
238--250 training frames a fold provides without transfer from natural images. The practical
reading is that this task is limited by how many distinct eggs have been touched, not by
initialisation or by the choice among recent YOLO variants.

\paragraph{The harder class is the one that matters.} The aggregate mAP$_{50}$ averages over both
classes and so flatters the task. Across every grouped run, whole eggs are recalled at
0.918 on average but cracked eggs at only 0.769, and in the worst fold cracked-egg recall
falls to 0.531 -- roughly half the cracks in that held-out set are missed. Average precision
shows the same ordering (0.896 against 0.844). On the released split the same ordering
appears but far more gently: cracked-egg recall averages 0.865 there and never falls below
0.692. The leaky split thus overstates the average and conceals the tail, which is the
failure mode that matters: in grading a missed crack ships a defective egg, while a false
alarm costs one good egg. Any deployment of this modality should be tuned on cracked-egg
recall rather than on mAP$_{50}$, and reported per class.
